The Gilligan assistant and its water-quality reports may only suggest possible causes, next steps and referrals that appear here with an approved status. Anything not approved stays hidden from customers: reports then show the threshold crossing without naming a cause, and chat describes what was measured and what the sensors cannot tell.

Approving an entry means the wording is defensible exactly as written, the conditions for showing it are right, and Clean Earth Rovers will stand behind it appearing in a customer-facing answer or report. Please mark each entry, and note any edits beside its wording.

Referrals to Clean Earth Rovers services also need their contact details confirmed. An entry with a referral stays hidden until both the entry and the referral are approved.

Sewage-like pattern in coastal water

sewage-marine · Possible cause · Report and chat

Conductivity fell while turbidity rose and dissolved oxygen and ORP declined. In coastal water this combination is consistent with fresh water carrying organic material, such as a sewage release. Stormwater runoff looks similar but coincides with rain. Sensor data alone cannot confirm the source.
Shown when
A report event, or tool results, show the full marine sewage pattern at a marine, brackish or estuarine pod.
Report selection
Sewage; marine water only; confidence at least 70%
Evidence needed
In the same window: dissolved oxygen down, ORP down, turbidity up and conductivity down.
Limitation
Cannot tell sewage from stormwater without a rainfall record, and cannot detect bacteria.
Sources
sot-v2 §6.2 (raw sewage spill row)
sot-v2 §6.4 (Raw Sewage Spill, Marine)

Low-oxygen pattern

hypoxia · Possible cause · Report and chat

Dissolved oxygen and ORP fell together while conductivity and turbidity held steady. This is consistent with oxygen being used up faster than it is replaced, a condition that can stress or kill fish.

A report event, or tool results, show dissolved oxygen and ORP falling together with no rise in conductivity or turbidity.

Warming with falling oxygen

thermal · Possible cause · Report and chat

Temperature rose while dissolved oxygen fell and the other readings held steady. Warmer water holds less oxygen, so this pattern is consistent with a warm-water discharge or with regional warming, such as a heat wave, or a marine heatwave in coastal water. Warming alone explains only a modest drop; a larger fall suggests oxygen is also being consumed.
Shown when
A report event, or tool results, show temperature up and dissolved oxygen down with no other reading moving.
Report selection
Thermal; any water; confidence at least 60%
Evidence needed
Temperature up and dissolved oxygen down in the same window; conductivity, pH, ORP and turbidity steady.
Limitation
One pod cannot tell a local discharge from regional warming; a heatwave affects a wide area and can last from days to months.
Sources
sot-v2 §6.2 (thermal discharge and marine heatwave rows)
sot-v2 §6.4 (Thermal Discharge)
sot-v2 §5.6

Rule out the instrument first

fault-first · Limitation · Report and chat

A sensor problem can look like a water-quality change. A single isolated point, a reading stuck at the same value for a long time, or every reading changing at the same instant are more likely signs of a sensor or connection problem. A real event more often shows a combination of readings that fits a physical explanation and lasts across several readings.
Shown when
A reading crossed a configured threshold, or the user asks why a reading changed suddenly.
Report selection

Evidence needed
A threshold crossing or an unexplained change.
Limitation
The report does not yet run automatic fault checks, so this is a caution, not a finding.

Turbidity is a relative clarity level

turbidity-relative · Limitation · Report and chat

Turbidity is shown as a relative clarity level (Clear, Moderate or Turbid), not a calibrated measurement, so it shows whether the water became cloudier but not by exactly how much. A long run of readings stuck at 0 (which looks like clear water) or at 1005 (which looks like very cloudy water) is the sensor's default for a bad reading or a failed sensor. Contact Clean Earth Rovers support to have the sensor replaced.
Shown when
A pattern involving turbidity was found, the user asks what a turbidity value means, or turbidity has stayed at 0 or 1005 for an extended period.
Report selection
Sewage, Stormwater; any water
Evidence needed
A turbidity reading or a turbidity change.
Limitation
A short run of 0 can be genuinely clear water; only a sustained run of the same value points to a failed sensor.

Oil spills and CER cleanup

cer-oil-spill-referral · Next step · Chat only

The pod's sensors largely cannot detect oil or fuel, so its readings cannot confirm or rule out a spill. If you see a sheen or smell fuel, report it. In the US, call the National Response Center (1-800-424-8802); in California, also call the Cal OES State Warning Center (1-800-852-7550). Elsewhere, contact your local environmental or maritime authority. Clean Earth Rovers can also help with small-to-medium spill cleanup.
Shown when
The user reports an oil sheen, a fuel smell or a spill, or asks whether the pod can detect oil.
Report selection
Chosen by the assistant from the conditions below
Evidence needed
The user's own report or question about oil.
Limitation
Oil barely changes the six measured readings.
Referral
Clean Earth Rovers: Oil spill cleanup (small to medium) (sales@cleanearthrovers.com, or your usual contact at Clean Earth Rovers if you already work with one) approved
