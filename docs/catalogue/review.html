Gilligan guidance review

Catalogue version 2026-09-19.1 · 38 entries: 0 approved, 38 draft, 0 rejected

The Gilligan assistant and its water-quality reports may only suggest possible causes, next steps and referrals that appear here with an approved status. Anything not approved stays hidden from customers: reports then show the threshold crossing without naming a cause, and chat describes what was measured and what the sensors cannot tell.

Approving an entry means the wording is defensible exactly as written, the conditions for showing it are right, and Clean Earth Rovers will stand behind it appearing in a customer-facing answer or report. Please mark each entry, and note any edits beside its wording.

Referrals to Clean Earth Rovers services also need their contact details confirmed. An entry with a referral stays hidden until both the entry and the referral are approved.

Sewage-like pattern in coastal water

sewage-marine · Possible cause · Report and chat

Conductivity fell while turbidity rose and dissolved oxygen and ORP declined. In coastal water this combination is consistent with fresh water carrying organic material, such as a sewage release. Stormwater runoff looks similar but coincides with rain. Sensor data alone cannot confirm the source.
Shown when
A report event, or tool results, show the full marine sewage pattern at a marine, brackish or estuarine pod.
Report selection
Sewage; marine water only; confidence at least 70%
Evidence needed
In the same window: dissolved oxygen down, ORP down, turbidity up and conductivity down.
Limitation
Cannot tell sewage from stormwater without a rainfall record, and cannot detect bacteria.
Sources
sot-v2 §6.2 (raw sewage spill row)
sot-v2 §6.4 (Raw Sewage Spill, Marine)
advice-drafts disc-sewage-edu-01

Low-oxygen pattern

hypoxia · Possible cause · Report and chat

Dissolved oxygen and ORP fell together while conductivity and turbidity held steady. This is consistent with oxygen being used up faster than it is replaced, a condition that can stress or kill fish.
Shown when
A report event, or tool results, show dissolved oxygen and ORP falling together with no rise in conductivity or turbidity.
Report selection
Hypoxia; any water; confidence at least 50%
Evidence needed
Dissolved oxygen and ORP both below this pod's configured thresholds in the same window; conductivity and turbidity not rising.
Limitation
A worn or fouled dissolved-oxygen sensor, or still water around it, can produce falsely low readings.
Sources
sot-v2 §6.2 (hypoxia or fish-kill row)

Natural cold-water events in coastal water

upwelling-caution · Limitation · Report and chat

In coastal water, cold, low-oxygen water rising from depth (upwelling) or arriving with internal tides can lower dissolved oxygen and pH naturally. If temperature fell at the same time, that natural explanation is more likely than pollution.
Shown when
Dissolved oxygen fell at a marine, brackish or estuarine pod, or the user asks whether low oxygen in coastal water means pollution.
Report selection
Hypoxia; marine water only
Evidence needed
A dissolved-oxygen decline at a coastal pod.
Limitation
Upwelling usually affects several nearby sites at once; one pod cannot show that.
Sources
sot-v2 §5.3
sot-v2 §5.4
sot-v2 §11 (temperature and DO coupling)

Warming with falling oxygen

thermal · Possible cause · Report and chat

Temperature rose while dissolved oxygen fell and the other readings held steady. Warmer water holds less oxygen, so this pattern is consistent with a warm-water discharge or with regional warming such as a marine heatwave.
Shown when
A report event, or tool results, show temperature up and dissolved oxygen down with no other reading moving.
Report selection
Thermal; any water; confidence at least 60%
Evidence needed
Temperature up and dissolved oxygen down in the same window; conductivity, pH, ORP and turbidity steady.
Limitation
One pod cannot tell a local discharge from regional warming; a heatwave affects a wide area and lasts days to weeks.
Sources
sot-v2 §6.2 (thermal discharge and marine heatwave rows)

algal-bloom · Possible cause · Report and chat

CER algal bloom cleanup

cer-algal-bloom-referral · Next step · Report (stakeholder) and chat

Clean Earth Rovers offers algal bloom cleanup.
Shown when
A report found the algae pattern, or the user describes a visible bloom (discolored water, scum or mats) and asks for help removing it.
Report selection
Algal bloom; any water; confidence at least 60%
Evidence needed
An algal bloom event, or the user's own description of a visible bloom.
Limitation
The sensors cannot confirm a bloom; this is a service offer, not a diagnosis.
Referral
Clean Earth Rovers: Algal bloom cleanup (info@cleanearthrovers.com, or book a call at https://calendar.app.google/b4asoD6b6vuae7Qj6) draft

Oil spills and CER response

cer-oil-spill-referral · Next step · Chat only

The pod's sensors largely cannot detect oil or fuel, so its readings cannot confirm or rule out a spill. Clean Earth Rovers offers oil spill response.
Shown when
The user reports an oil sheen, a fuel smell or a spill, or asks whether the pod can detect oil.
Report selection
Chosen by the assistant from the conditions below
Evidence needed
The user's own report or question about oil.
Limitation
Oil barely changes the six measured readings.
Referral
Clean Earth Rovers: Oil spill response (info@cleanearthrovers.com, or book a call at https://calendar.app.google/b4asoD6b6vuae7Qj6) draft
