Flagging: the whole story

Algalita Pod, Michael's decisions of 2026-10-12, a customer's first chat, and one plan for every flagging problem so far. Written 2026-10-12 on branch task/flagging-share-window (from dev 60f531c). Nothing here is deployed.

In one minute. Gilligan has two opposite habits that both hurt trust. It raises alarms too easily (one or three odd readings became "needs immediate attention"), and when it sees something unusual it blames the sensor ("most likely a pH probe malfunction", "data quality is the biggest concern"). Michael wants the opposite of both: stay quiet about brief blips, flag only sustained problems, and treat the data as real unless the device itself says otherwise.

The data supports Michael on how Gilligan should talk: the probe reported no fault, and the other four sensors behaved normally all month. It does not settle what happened to pH on Sep 25-29: pH jumped by 4-5 units within half an hour while nothing else moved, which is very unusual for seawater. Only a hand-meter comparison or CER's service record can settle that, and the customer's team is about to do exactly that kind of testing.

The proposed fix gives every reading one of four labels (normal, out of range, unusual, device fault), flags a metric only when out-of-range readings reach 5% of a rolling 24 hours, and keeps the word "fault" for faults the device itself reports.

Analogy: a smoke alarm in a kitchen. A good alarm ignores a single puff from the toaster (a brief blip), goes off when smoke keeps building (a sustained problem), and stops once the air clears (clearing). When it sees something strange, like a reading that makes no sense, it should say "this looks odd, worth checking the alarm", not "your alarm is broken, ignore it". Gilligan today goes off at the toaster and tells you the alarm is broken.

What the numbers can and cannot prove

Points to the measurement
Other channels identical on odd and normal nights; ORP within a few mV while pH moves 4-5 units; pH high one night, low the next; back to normal around Sep 29-30, when the registry's next calibration date (Sep 30) fell.
Would point to real water
ORP moving against pH by tens or hundreds of mV; a step in conductivity or oxygen at the same moment; v2's alkaline signature (concrete washout) raises conductivity as well as pH.
Cannot be shown from data
Whether the probe was wrong. Only a hand-meter comparison or CER's service record (was the probe recalibrated or swapped around Sep 30?) settles it.

5. The proposed solution

One idea runs through all of it: report what the water did, say plainly what is unusual, and call something a fault only when the device says so.

Every reading gets one label

Normal

Inside the limit, or beyond it by no more than the noise cushion.

Out of range

Beyond the limit by more than the cushion. The only label that counts toward a flag.

Unusual

A spike (3 x MAD), a jump faster than v2's rate-of-change guide, or pH outside 3-12. Shown and described; never counts toward a flag; never called a fault. Wording: "very unusual for seawater; a calibration check would confirm."

Device fault

The device said so: an error flag, a read failure, a reading at exactly 0 or 14, a flat line, stuck turbidity, every channel failing at once. Left out; "sensor fault" and "inspect" are allowed here and only here.

When a metric is flagged

  1. Share rule (Michael #1, #2): out-of-range readings reach 5% of the kept readings in some rolling 24 hours, and number at least 3. At 48 readings a day that is 3; the minimum of 3 stops a sparse window (1 of 10) from flagging. The run-of-3 stops being the trigger.
  2. Per day (Michael #5): a report or a multi-day answer names the days with a flag, not the whole period.
  3. Current or past (Michael #4): a flag is current only if the latest 24 hours meet the rule. A past flag is told in past tense, with no "urgent" or "action required".
  4. Action Required: only for a current flag beyond the limit by more than 15% of the range (or the cushion, if larger).
  5. Edge rule (Michael #6): a metric within one cushion of its limit for at least 2 hours on at least half the days of the period is mentioned ("sat at the low edge of its range most afternoons") and lifts the report to Watch at most. A share of a single 24 hours would miss a daily dip that lasts a few hours, which is exactly Michael's pod.

Everything else

  • A data issue comes only from device faults (3 or more in a row) and covers only those readings, so it can no longer hide out-of-range readings beside it.
  • Report events come from the flagged 24-hour windows, so an event and a flag never disagree.
  • Chat and report keep one shared rule file, as today.
  • Not touched: alert emails (Michael's open question), conductivity fallbacks (question 24), site baselines (section 9).

7. Decisions for you

  1. Readings outside pH 3-12: label them unusual and keep showing them (recommended), remove the cutoff so they count as out of range, or keep calling them faults (today). Reverses the approved "pH 2.07 stays implausible" guard either way.
  2. Add v2's rate-of-change test (recommended): pH 0.3 per 10 minutes, scaled to the gap between readings, for marine and brackish pods; temperature 2 °C per 10 minutes. Freshwater pH gets no rate test, because v2 gives none.
  3. Unusual readings never count toward a flag (recommended), the same as the spikes Michael agreed to ignore.
  4. Share rule: 5% of a rolling 24 hours and at least 3 readings (recommended); run-of-3 retired as the trigger.
  5. Noise cushion: v2's minimum detectable change, pH 0.2 (recommended), or 2 x the upper accuracy, pH 0.4.
  6. Action Required: current flags only, beyond 15% of the range (recommended); the 15% is this project's number, not a source's.
  7. Edge rule: what "extended" means. Recommended: within one cushion of the limit for at least 2 hours on at least half the days, mentioned plus Watch at most. Half of a single 24 hours would miss Michael's afternoon dips.
  8. Data issues: device faults only, scoped to their own readings (recommended).
  9. Report events from flagged windows (recommended, your earlier lean).
  10. Paid replay: the customer's two questions, 3 runs on dev now and 3 on this branch after the fix, about $0.30.

9. "Learn normal": later, not now

Michael asked whether Gilligan could learn what is normal as data accumulates. It can, with plain statistics rather than model training: for each pod, the typical value and spread at each hour of the day over its own last 2-4 weeks (v2 §4 and §9.1).

  • Gains: "unusual for this site at this hour" instead of generic ranges; a better spike test than today's whole-series MAD; v2's climatology test becomes possible.
  • Risks: a baseline learned from a bad stretch makes that stretch look normal; new pods have no history; moved pods (Algalita moved in August) need history cut at the move; it changes what "unusual" means everywhere, so it needs its own evaluation.
  • Where: POST_LAUNCH_BACKLOG.md, after this flagging work.
