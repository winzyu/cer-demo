• Do not insert an electrode or other material into a bottle
containing stock buffer solution—always pour the buf-
fer into a separate container and discard the solution
after use.
• Do not contaminate the buffer or sample by placing a wet
or contaminated electrode into the solution to be measured
(this can happen, for example, if drops of deionized
water (DIW) used to clean the electrode drip into the
buffer or if buffer solution drips into the sample). To
minimize contamination or dilution, rinse the electrode
with the solution to be measured by slowly pouring
the sample or buffer to be measured over the electrode.
Then place the electrode into the vessel containing the
sample or buffer, remove the electrode, and dispose of
the sample or buffer. Repeat this procedure for a total
of three times every time a measurement is made. To
measure the sample pH, add fresh sample or buffer to
the same vessel and then place the electrode directly
into the measurement vessel. This procedure ensures
that the pH electrode and measurement vessel are well
rinsed prior to measurement.
• Protect buffers against wide temperature variations,
whether they are in transit, in use, or in storage. Never
expose buffers to extreme heat or freezing tempera-
tures. If buffers experience these conditions, their pH
values can no longer be assumed to be valid and they
should be discarded appropriately.
3.0 	Maintenance and Preparation of
pH Instruments
Proper care of pH meters, and particularly of the elec-
trode, is essential for maintaining the accuracy and precision
required for pH measurements and increases the longevity of
the equipment. Maintenance includes adhering to the manufac-
turer's instructions for the use and care of the pH instrument
system, routine electrode cleaning, reconditioning, and storage
requirements. As always, follow the manufacturer’s instruc-
tions for the specific type of electrode in use.
pH-meter and electrode performance is required to be
checked before each water-quality field trip and at each
field site.
3.1 	Electrode Care and Cleaning
USGS field personnel should integrate the following
guidance for the care and cleaning of pH electrodes into their
routine field-measurement procedures.
• Never handle the glass bulb with bare fingers. Oily film
or scratches on the bulb will interfere with the design
characteristics of the glass bulb membrane and affect
pH measurements.
• Inspect the electrode and electrode cable for:
• cut or frayed cable(s)
• broken connectors and mismatched or missing parts
• a visibly scratched or broken glass bulb, cracked
electrode body, and broken or damaged internal
electrodes (reference and measurement electrodes)
• a visibly contaminated reference junction
• low filling solution
• Periodically remove and replace the filling solution
• Gel-filled electrodes do not require filling and typically
require less maintenance than liquid-filled electrodes.
• Do not store gel-filled electrodes in low-ionic-
strength water, including DIW, even temporarily,
as ions will leach from the gel into the dilute water,
causing dilution of the filling gel and errors in pH

• Use at least two pH buffer solutions with documented,
NIST-traceable pH values that bracket the sample pH
for accurate calibration of the pH instrument system.
• If a large primary source of pH buffer is purchased
initially, use a secondary bottle to store pH buffers
while in the field to minimize the chance of contami-
nating the main pH buffer source. Pour the amount of
each buffer needed from the source container into a
clean polyethylene bottle dedicated for the respective
buffer, and label the bottle with the buffer’s pH value,
lot number, and expiration date. Keep a printed chart
of the temperature-adjusted pH values provided by the
manufacturer for that buffer.
• A calibration check of the pH meter’s temperature
sensor is required to be performed at least annually
(NFM A6.1). Most single-analyte pH meters use a
thermistor built into the electrode. Multiparameter
instruments typically have the temperature sensor
in a different part of the instrument system than the
pH electrode.
• The pH instrumentation should be calibrated daily
and recalibrated if a check of either of the two pH
buffers nearest to the pH of the sample at a temperature
that is ± 10 °C of the sample deviates by more than
± 0.05 pH units.
• The isopotential point is the characteristic pH where
the e.m.f. is equal to 0 mV. Most modern pH electrodes
are designed to have an isopotential point at or near
pH=7. Test the electrode when it is new by placing it
in pH-7.00 buffer and record the voltage. Repeat this
test regularly and verify that the value is ±15 mV of
the original value. If the isopotential point is more than
±15 mV of the original value, clean, recondition, or
replace the electrode.
Do not use pH buffers that have exceeded their date of
expiration. Buffers that have been transferred to a second-
ary bottle for use while in the field should be refreshed
before every field trip.
4.1 	Calibration Procedure
The following calibration procedure is expected to yield
accurate sample-pH measurements for waters with specific
conductance less than 60,000 μS/cm, temperatures from 0 to
90 °C, and pH in the range from 1.68 to 10. Standard buffer
solutions and combination pH electrode can be appropri-
ately used to achieve an accurate measurement of the pH
of environmental waters that is within these environmental
ranges. Studies of waters with conditions that fall outside
the ranges described above may require pH calibration and
measurement modifications.
4.1.1 	Calibration of a Single-Parameter Meter
and pH Electrode used for Sample Measurement
or an Alkalinity Titration
1. 	Under most situations, it is recommended to calibrate
the pH meter and electrode in a controlled environment
such as a laboratory rather than in the field. Most mod-
ern meters automatically adjust pH measurements for
changes in temperature, and these corrections are usually
accurate within a temperature range of 5 to 35 °C and for
pH measurements between 4.0 and 9.5. Electrodes may
not behave ideally, however, and this feature should be
tested regularly with measurements on buffers equili-
brated to ambient field temperatures.
