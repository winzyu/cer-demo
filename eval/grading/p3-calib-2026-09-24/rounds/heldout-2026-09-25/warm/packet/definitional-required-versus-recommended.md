## Turn 1

**Question:** Reading through these chapters, some steps are flagged as required and others as recommended. Practically speaking, what's the difference in what we have to do?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- states that 'required' marks established policy, settled by research or by technical consensus among specialists, and is to be followed as written
- states that 'recommended' means more than one acceptable way of doing it exists
- states that when a recommended step is involved, the reason for picking the option you picked has to be written down

**Must not**
- treats the two words as interchangeable emphasis or as stylistic wording
- claims 'recommended' means optional and needs no record
- invents a third category or a numeric grading scheme

**Should cite**
- usgs-nfm-a6.0-field-measurement-guidelines.pdf
- usgs-nfm-a6.3-specific-conductance.pdf

### Answer A

“Required” steps are those that USGS policy mandates; the manual says they “indicates that USGS Water Mission Area (WMA) policy has been established on the basis of research”【1†"indicates that USGS Water Mission Area (WMA) policy has been established on the basis of research"】 and any departure “must be independently quality assured and documented”【1†"must be independently quality assured and documented"】.  

“Recommended” steps are those where the manual allows several acceptable alternatives; it notes they “indicates that, on the basis of research or consensus, there are several acceptable alternatives to a given procedure”【1†"indicates that, on the basis of research or consensus, there are several acceptable alternatives to a given procedure"】 and, likewise, any deviation “must be independently quality assured and documented”【1†"must be independently quality assured and documented"】.

### Answer B

“Required” steps are those that USGS policy has formally established and that field personnel must follow as part of the official protocol【6†"The terms “require,” “required,” and “requirements” in reference to USGS protocols indicate that USGS Water Mission Area (WMA) policy has been established on the basis of research or consensus"】.  

“Recommended” steps are not mandatory; they represent alternatives that are acceptable based on professional judgment and project needs, and can be chosen or omitted as long as the choice is documented【6†"The terms “recommend,” “recommended,” and “recommendation” indicate that… there are several acceptable alternatives to a given procedure or equipment selection"】.  

In practice, you must perform all required actions, while recommended actions are optional and may be applied when appropriate.

## Turn 2

**Question:** And if a site just won't let us do the required one - no room for the gear, say - is it enough to note it in the log and move on?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- states that a departure from a required procedure has to be documented
- states that documenting is not sufficient on its own - the departure also has to be independently quality assured
- notes that the same documentation-plus-independent-quality-assurance expectation applies to departures from recommended procedures

**Must not**
- says a note in the field log by itself closes the matter
- invents a specific USGS form, approval chain, or sign-off role for the departure
- claims required procedures can never be departed from under any circumstances

**Should cite**
- usgs-nfm-a6.3-specific-conductance.pdf
- usgs-nfm-a6.0-field-measurement-guidelines.pdf

### Answer A

The manual states that if you cannot follow a required step, the departure “must be independently quality assured and documented”【1†"must be independently quality assured and documented"】. Simply noting it in the log without the required quality‑assurance documentation would not satisfy the protocol.

<sub>Context supplied: 3 chunk(s) from 2 document(s) — https://pubs.usgs.gov/tm/09/a6.0/tm9a6.0.pdf, https://pubs.usgs.gov/tm/09/a6.3/tm9-a6_3.pdf. Full text: `context/definitional-required-versus-recommended/turn2-A.txt`</sub>

### Answer B

No. Required steps can’t simply be skipped and recorded as “done”. If a required action can’t be performed (for example, because the site won’t allow the gear), USGS policy says the deviation must be **independently quality‑assured and documented** rather than just noted in the log and ignored【6†"Departure from or modifications to stipulated requirements, if necessary for accomplishing specific data‑quality requirements or study objectives, must be independently quality assured and documented"】.  

In practice you would need to record the reason the step couldn’t be done, obtain the appropriate QA/QC verification (often by a supervisor or water‑quality specialist), and document the deviation in the instrument logbook before proceeding. Simply noting it in the log without that independent QA is not sufficient.
