## Turn 1

**Question:** Dumb question, but when I calibrate the acidity sensor can I just put it straight into the bottle the solution came in? Feels wasteful pouring it out every time.

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- States that the electrode must never be put into the stock bottle
- States that solution is poured into a separate clean vessel for the measurement
- States that used solution is discarded and never poured back into the stock bottle
- Recommends decanting into a dedicated secondary bottle for field use, labelled with its value, lot number and expiration date

**Must not**
- Tells the operator it is acceptable to immerse the electrode in the stock bottle
- Tells the operator used solution may be returned to the bottle
- Answers about probe storage solution and the transport cap instead of the calibration standards

**Should cite**
- usgs-nfm-a6.4-ph.pdf

### Answer A

No. When calibrating a pH (acidity) probe you should not place the electrode directly into the bottle that holds the stock buffer. The recommended practice is to pour the buffer into a clean, separate container, use it for the calibration, and then discard that solution【6†"Do not insert an electrode or other material into a bottle containing stock buffer solution—always pour the buffer into a separate container and discard the solution after use."】

No. USGS guidance says you should never place the electrode directly into a bottle that holds the stock buffer solution; instead, pour the buffer into a separate container and discard the solution after use【2†"Do not insert an electrode or other material into a bottle containing stock buffer solution—always pour the buffer into a separate container and discard the solution after use."】

## Turn 2

**Question:** The decanted bottles live in the truck. How long can I keep using those before I throw them out?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- States that the decanted field bottles should be refreshed before every field trip
- States that solution past its expiration date must not be used
- States that the solutions must be protected from wide temperature swings and from extreme heat or freezing
- States that solution which has been frozen or overheated can no longer be assumed valid and should be discarded
- Notes that the lot number, expiration date and the temperature at use are recorded in the instrument logbook

**Must not**
- Gives a specific shelf life in days or weeks that the manual does not state
- Treats a truck as acceptable storage without the temperature caveat
- Invents a temperature range for storage

**Should cite**
- usgs-nfm-a6.4-ph.pdf

### Answer A

You can keep re‑using the decanted buffer bottles only until the buffer’s expiration date is reached. The guidance says to label each buffer container with the date it was opened **and the expiration date**, and to discard any buffer that is past its expiration【5†"Label pH buffer containers with the date opened and the expiration date. Do not use expired buffers."】. If a bottle has no printed expiration date, you should follow the manufacturer’s recommended shelf life and replace the buffer once that time has elapsed.

### Answer B

USGS procedure requires that any buffer you’ve transferred to a secondary bottle for field use be refreshed before each field trip. In other words, you shouldn’t keep using the same decanted bottle for multiple trips without replacing the buffer; you must discard or refresh it at least once per trip【3†"Buffers that have been transferred to a secondary bottle for use while in the field should be refreshed before every field trip."】. Additionally, if the buffer has passed its expiration date or has been exposed to extreme temperatures, it should be discarded【2†"Protect buffers against wide temperature variations... If buffers experience these conditions, their pH values can no longer be assumed to be valid and they should be discarded appropriately."】.
