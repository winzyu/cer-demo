## Turn 1

**Question:** When I drop the sonde in, the numbers wobble for a bit. How steady do they need to get before the reading is worth writing down, and how long am I meant to stand there?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- gives the temperature criterion as about plus or minus 0.2 C
- gives the specific conductance criterion as about plus or minus 5 microsiemens per centimetre at or below 100, and about plus or minus 3 percent above that
- gives the dissolved oxygen criterion as about plus or minus 0.2 mg/L
- gives the pH criterion as about plus or minus 0.1 unit
- Gives turbidity stabilization as ±0.5 TU or ±5 percent, whichever is greater, at or below 100 TU; above 100 TU uses ±10 percent
- states the criterion is judged over five or more consecutive readings, not a single pair
- gives the minimum wait in surface water as at least 60 seconds for the sensors and sonde body to reach thermal equilibrium
- states 60 seconds is a minimum and some instruments need longer per the manufacturer, up to several minutes depending on the temperature step

**Must not**
- invents a stabilisation threshold for any parameter
- gives a single tolerance covering all parameters
- presents the 60-second wait as a maximum or as sufficient in all cases
- confuses the stabilisation criteria with sensor accuracy specifications

**Should cite**
- usgs-nfm-a6.0-field-measurement-guidelines.pdf
- usgs-nfm-a6.8-multiparameter-instruments.pdf

### Answer A

When you lower the sonde, keep taking measurements until the values stop “wobbling.” The field manuals say you should consider the reading stable only after a series of sequential measurements meet a stability criterion. For example, stability is reached when three – five (or more) consecutive readings taken at regular intervals are all within 10 % of each other【44†"Stability is reached if values for three (for in situ procedure) to five (for flowthrough‑chamber procedure) or more sequential readings, spaced at regular time increments, are within 10 percent"}; alternatively, you can record at least five sets of measurements spaced 3–5 minutes apart and then use the median of the last five (or more) readings【18†"Record at least five sets of measurements at intervals of 3 to 5 minutes. Report the median of the last five or more readings"}.

So, wait until the readings meet one of those criteria—typically a few minutes (3–5 min per set) of observation—before writing the value down.

### Answer B

When you lower the sonde you should let the sensors come to thermal equilibrium and then keep taking readings until the variability between consecutive measurements falls within the USGS stabilization criteria. The manual says the “variability among consecutive measurements does not exceed an established criterion… for a set of about five (or more) consecutive measurements”【3†"variability among consecutive measurements does not exceed an established criterion"} and that “Surface water: Allow at least 60 seconds… for sensors to equilibrate… Take instrument readings until the stabilization criteria in table 6.0‑1 are met. Record the median of the final three or more readings as the value to be reported”【3†"Allow at least 60 seconds (or follow the manufacturer’s guidelines) for sensors to equilibrate… Record the median of the final three or more readings"}.

The specific numeric limits for each parameter are listed in the stabilization‑criteria tables, for example:

* Temperature ± 0.2 °C【5†"Temperature ± 0.2 °C"}  
* Specific electrical conductance ± 5 μS/cm (≤ 100 μS/cm) or ± 3 % ( > 100 μS/cm)【5†"Specific electrical conductance (SC) ± 5 μS/cm for SC ≤100 μS/cm, or ± 3% for SC >100 μS/cm"}  
* Dissolved oxygen ± 0.2 mg/L【5†"DO ± 0.2 mg/L"}  
* pH ± 0.1 pH units【5†"pH ± 0.1 pH units"}  
* Turbidity ± 0.5 TU or 5 % of the measured value (whichever is greater) for ≤ 100 TU, or 10 % for > 100 TU【5†"Turbidity ± 0.5 TU or 5% of the measured value, whichever is greater, for turbidity ≤ 100 TU; or 10% of the measured value for turbidity > 100 TU"}.

So, after the initial ~60‑second equilibration, keep watching the display until the readings for each parameter stay within those ± limits for at least five successive measurements; then you can record the median of the last three (or more) stable values.

## Turn 2

**Question:** Someone on the team says the multiparameter chapter has its own numbers and they're stricter than the general ones. Is that right, and which do I follow?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- states plainly that the two tables give the same stabilisation criteria and do not conflict
- states the general field-measurement chapter itself points multiparameter users to the multiparameter chapter for criteria of the same kind
- states the criteria are standard sensor stabilisation criteria and that actual sensor accuracy varies by model and manufacturer, which is a separate matter
- For persistent variability, extends equilibration and records additional measurements, then either reports the median of the final five or more readings or selects a representative final reading using site knowledge and professional judgment, documenting the decision

**Must not**
- invents a discrepancy between the two tables
- claims one chapter is stricter than the other
- Changes a correctly stated criterion without source support; correcting an earlier incomplete or incorrect criterion is allowed
- resolves an imagined conflict by picking a document instead of stating that they agree

**Should cite**
- usgs-nfm-a6.8-multiparameter-instruments.pdf
- usgs-nfm-a6.0-field-measurement-guidelines.pdf

### Answer A

Yes. The USGS multiparameter‑instrument chapter (A6.8) contains its own “stabilization criteria for common field parameters” that are separate from the more general field‑measurement guidance【17†"Stabilization criteria for common field parameters】. When you are using a multiparameter sonde, you should follow the criteria laid out in that chapter rather than the broader, non‑instrument‑specific recommendations.

### Answer B

Both the general field‑measurement guidance (Table 6.0‑1) and the multiparameter‑instrument chapter (Table 6.8‑5) list the same stabilization limits for the individual parameters. For example, the multiparameter table specifies temperature ± 0.2 °C, specific electrical conductance ± 5 μS/cm (≤ 100 μS/cm) or ± 3 % (> 100 μS/cm), dissolved‑oxygen ± 0.2 mg/L, pH ± 0.1 pH units, and turbidity ± 0.5 TU or 5 % (≤ 100 TU) or 10 % (> 100 TU)【5†"Temperature ± 0.2 °C"}【5†"Specific electrical conductance (SC) ± 5 μS/cm for SC ≤100 μS/cm, or ± 3% for SC >100 μS/cm"}【5†"DO ± 0.2 mg/L"}【5†"pH ± 0.1 pH units"}【5†"Turbidity ± 0.5 TU or 5% of the measured value, whichever is greater, for turbidity ≤ 100 TU; or 10% of the measured value for turbidity > 100 TU"}.

The earlier “general” guidance (Table 6.0‑1) uses the same criteria, stating that variability among consecutive measurements must stay within those limits for about five or more readings【3†"variability among consecutive measurements does not exceed an established criterion"} and that you should allow at least 60 seconds for equilibration before checking those limits【3†"Allow at least 60 seconds (or follow the manufacturer’s guidelines) for sensors to equilibrate… Take instrument readings until the stabilization criteria in table 6.0‑1 are met"}.

Therefore, the multiparameter chapter does not impose stricter numbers; it reiterates the same USGS stabilization criteria. You should follow the stabilization limits as given in either table—they are equivalent.
