4.0 	Reporting
USGS personnel are instructed to enter the DO value on
field forms or into electronic records indicating the method
code (optical, spectrophotometric, other) appropriate for the
method used for DO measurement.
DO concentrations for the optical-sensor method are
reported to the nearest 0.1 mg/L and stored to the nearest
0.01 mg/L.
DO concentrations for the spectrophotometric/Rhodazine
D and Indigo Carmine methods are reported and stored to the
nearest 0.01 mg/L.
Note that the percentage of DO saturation in water can
be greater than 100 as a result of nonequilibrium conditions
induced by photosynthesis and oxygen exchange with the
atmosphere. When the concentration exceeds 20 mg/L, check
the sensor manufacturer’s specifications and:
• Report “> the listed numerical limit” if the concentra-
tion exceeds the manufacturer’s specified sensor range.
5.0 	Correction Factors for Oxygen
Solubility and Salinity
Solubility concentrations of oxygen in freshwater at
various temperatures and pressures (table 6.2–2, at end of
chapter) and correction factors for salinity based on specific
conductance (table 6.2–4, at end of chapter) were generated
from the equations of Benson and Krause (1980, 1984) and
can be customized to cover the range and number of decimal
places needed; see U.S. Geological Survey Office of Water
Quality Technical Memorandum 2011.03 (U.S. Geological
Survey, 2011). By accessing “DOTABLES” at https://water.
usgs.gov/software/DOTABLES/, the interactive software that
generated tables 6.2–2 and 6.2–4 (at end of chapter) the user
can self-generate individual values or tables of a specific range
of oxygen-solubility and salinity correction factors.
To adjust freshwater oxygen-solubility values for the
effects of salinity, use correction factors based on specific
conductance. Report the corrected values. Refer to the manu-
facturer’s instructions for the DO instrument before applying
a salinity correction. Most multiparameter instruments that
include DO and specific-conductance sensors automatically
correct the DO reading for salinity if needed, unless the fea-
ture is turned off by the user.
Correcting DO solubility for saline waters (specific
conductance greater than 2,000 μS/cm) varies with instru-
ment type, calibration method, and the salts in solution. The
correction based on specific conductance (table 6.2–4, at end
of chapter) is useful because accurate conductivity can be
determined easily from a field measurement.
