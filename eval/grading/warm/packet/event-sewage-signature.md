## Turn 1

**Question:** DO and ORP are both crashing, EC has risen and turbidity is up. What is this?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- matches the sewage/sanitary-discharge signature: DO down strongly, ORP down strongly, EC up, turbidity up, pH down
- notes the discriminator that it is not tied to time of day
- states that no single parameter diagnoses an event — the combination does

**Must not**
- names an event whose signature contradicts the given directions, such as thermal discharge or saltwater intrusion
- invents a signature

**Should cite**
- water-quality-metrics-source-of-truth.pdf

### Answer A

The pattern you’re describing—DO and ORP both dropping sharply, EC rising, and turbidity increasing—matches the **sewage / sanitary discharge** signature in the pollution‑event matrix.  
In that scenario, an organic‑laden discharge (e.g., sewage) consumes dissolved oxygen (DO crash), drives the redox potential down (ORP crash), adds ions to the water (EC rise), and carries suspended solids (turbidity rise) 【1】【2】.  

This is a classic indicator of an organic pollution event. If you’re concerned about water quality or safety, you should consult local public‑health or environmental authorities, as the sensor alone cannot determine whether the water is safe to swim or drink.

### Answer B

The combination of a sharp drop in dissolved oxygen (DO) and oxidation‑reduction potential (ORP), a rise in electrical conductivity (EC), and an increase in turbidity is the classic signature of a **sewage or sanitary discharge** event. In the event‑signature matrix, this pattern is listed as:

- DO ↓↓  
- ORP ↓↓  
- EC ↑  
- Turbidity ↑  
- (often pH ↓)  

and is described as “DO crash + ORP crash + EC rise + turbidity rise, not tied to time of day”【2】. This indicates an organic‑laden, oxygen‑consuming discharge that also brings dissolved ions and suspended solids into the water.

## Turn 2

**Question:** Could it be an algal bloom instead?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- distinguishes the bloom signature: large in-phase daily DO and pH oscillations, midday supersaturation and a pre-dawn crash
- notes the described pattern is a sustained non-diel crash, which does not fit a bloom
- notes the pre-dawn DO minimum is the bloom danger window

**Must not**
- accepts algal bloom as an equally good fit without addressing the diel pattern

**Should cite**
- water-quality-metrics-source-of-truth.pdf

### Answer A

An algal bloom would show a **different signature**.  
Typical bloom indicators are:

* a temperature rise,  
* a DO crash that occurs just before dawn (the “pre‑dawn” dip),  
* in‑phase daily oscillations of DO and pH, and  
* no consistent rise in EC or turbidity 【2】.

The pattern you described—DO and ORP both dropping sharply, EC increasing, and turbidity rising—matches the **sewage / sanitary discharge** signature in the event matrix, not the algal bloom pattern. So it is unlikely to be an algal bloom.

### Answer B

An algal bloom has a different signature.  In the event‑signature matrix it is shown as:

- **Temperature ↑** (often high during the day)  
- **DO crash** that occurs just before dawn (the “pre‑dawn” dip)  
- **pH oscillations** that are in‑phase with the DO swings  
- Turbidity may rise, but it is usually a **secondary** effect from suspended algae, not a primary driver.  
- **EC** typically stays flat or may even drop slightly because the bloom is mainly organic matter, not a large ionic load.

The pattern you described—**DO and ORP both crashing, EC rising, and turbidity up**—matches the classic “sewage / sanitary discharge” signature (↓↓ DO, ↓↓ ORP, ↑ EC, ↑ turbidity) and is not characteristic of an algal bloom【2】【5】. So it is unlikely to be an algal bloom.

### Answer C

An algal bloom would usually show a **temperature rise** (the bloom heats the water) and **pH oscillations** (photosynthesis raises pH during the day and respiration lowers it at night). The pattern you described—DO and ORP dropping sharply, EC rising, and turbidity increasing—matches the “sewage / sanitary discharge” signature in the reference matrix (see the “Sewage / sanitary discharge” row in the event‑signature matrix, which lists ↓DO, ↓ORP, ↑EC, ↑turbidity). Because you didn’t mention a temperature spike or pH swings, the evidence points more strongly to a sewage or organic‑loading discharge rather than an algal bloom.
