## Turn 1

**Question:** A tech says our pH probe has "KCl creep" — what is that, and is the probe ruined?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- explains KCl creep as dried KCl residue from the storage solution forming a salt channel that wicks water out of the soaker bottle
- states plainly that the probe is not damaged
- gives the remedy: rinse the probe with water and carry on

**Must not**
- recommends replacing the probe
- states the probe is damaged or its calibration is permanently lost

**Should cite**
- IpH_probe.pdf

### Answer A

**KCl creep** is a phenomenon where potassium chloride (KCl) in the probe’s storage solution forms a salt channel that “wicks” water out of the soaker bottle, often when the probe is kept in a hot environment (e.g., during shipping). The result is that the probe’s storage solution dries out.  

The probe is **not ruined**. The documentation says you can simply rinse the probe with water and it will continue to function normally【1】【2】【3】.

### Answer B

**KCl creep** is the slow migration of potassium chloride (KCl) from the probe’s storage solution into the probe’s internal “soaker” bottle. The salt forms a channel that can wick water out of the bottle, especially if the probe is left dry or exposed to heat during shipping. This can make the probe appear dry or “dead” when it’s actually just dehydrated.

**Is the probe ruined?**  
No. KCl creep does not damage the probe’s electronics or sensing element. The probe can be re‑hydrated by rinsing it with water and, if desired, adding a small amount of KCl (or NaCl) to the soaker bottle. After re‑hydration the probe will function normally again.  

*Source: Atlas Scientific “pH Probe” documentation – sections on KCl creep and rehydration instructions.*

### Answer C

**KCl creep** is the migration of potassium chloride (KCl) from the probe’s storage solution into the probe’s internal “soaker” bottle.  
When this happens, the KCl forms a salt channel that wicks water out of the bottle, causing the probe to dry out.  
The probe itself is **not damaged**—the manufacturer says it can dry out for up to three weeks without harm. The fix is simply to re‑hydrate the probe (rinse it with water, add a small amount of KCl or NaCl, and let it sit for 15–20 min)【1】【12】.  

So, KCl creep is a normal, reversible drying issue; the probe is not ruined.

## Turn 2

**Question:** It was dry for about two weeks — same answer?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- states this pH probe can be left dry for up to 3 weeks without damage
- notes repeated wetting and drying cycles will eventually damage it and it should be kept wet
- gives the rehydration steps: tap water for 3–5 minutes, add a scoop of KCl (or NaCl), soak 15–20 minutes

**Must not**
- declares the probe dead after two weeks dry
- invents a different rehydration procedure

**Should cite**
- IpH_probe.pdf

### Answer A

Yes – the same applies.  
A dry probe for up to about three weeks does **not** mean it’s ruined. The Atlas Scientific industrial pH probe is designed to tolerate a dry period of up to 3 weeks without damage. If it’s been dry for two weeks, simply re‑hydrate it (e.g., soak in tap water, add a small amount of KCl or NaCl, and let it sit for 15–20 min) and it will work normally again【1】【2】. The “KCl creep” issue just means the storage solution can dry out, but the probe itself remains functional.

### Answer B

A two‑week dry period is within the probe’s tolerance. Atlas Scientific notes that a pH probe can be left dry for up to **three weeks without damage**【4‑8】.  
KCl creep simply means the salt in the storage solution has migrated and may have drawn some water out of the soaker bottle, so the probe looks “dry.” It does **not** harm the sensor’s electronics or glass bulb.

**What to do**

1. **Re‑hydrate** – place the probe in a beaker of tap water for 3–5 min.  
2. Add a small amount of KCl (or NaCl) to the water; the salt helps re‑establish the proper ionic environment.  
3. Let it sit for 15–20 min, then remove and rinse the probe.  
4. Re‑attach the storage solution cap and the probe is ready to use again.

So the probe is not ruined; it just needs to be re‑hydrated.

### Answer C

Yes. A probe that has been dry for two weeks is still within the “up to three weeks” window that the manufacturer says is safe. The probe isn’t damaged; you just need to re‑hydrate it (rinse, add a small amount of KCl or NaCl, and let it sit for 15–20 min)【1】【12】.
