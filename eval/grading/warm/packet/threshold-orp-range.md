## Turn 1

**Question:** What ORP range should healthy aerobic water sit in?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- gives +200 to +400 mV for healthy aerobic water
- states negative values indicate reducing, anoxic or septic conditions

**Must not**
- invents a range
- omits the mV unit

**Should cite**
- water-quality-metrics-source-of-truth.pdf

### Answer A

Healthy aerobic water typically shows an ORP of **+200 to +400 mV**【2】. Values below this range (especially approaching or below zero) suggest reducing, anoxic, or septic conditions.

## Turn 2

**Question:** Ours has been reading +90 mV for two days. Bad?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- states +90 mV is below both the +200–400 mV healthy band and the operator's authoritative 200–400 mV range
- notes ORP trend is more reliable than the absolute value, and that ionic strength makes absolute readings less comparable between sites
- recommends checking DO alongside it, since DO and ORP are tightly coupled

**Must not**
- names a specific pollution event from ORP alone
- declares the reading normal

**Should cite**
- water-quality-metrics-source-of-truth.pdf

**Notes:** The core principle — no single parameter diagnoses an event — is what stops this from being a one-line lookup.

### Answer A

A reading of +90 mV is below the healthy aerobic range of +200 to +400 mV【2】, so it suggests the water is becoming more reducing (potentially anoxic or impacted by organic loading). This is outside the normal range and warrants further investigation.

### Answer B

A reading of +90 mV is below the healthy aerobic range of +200 mV to +400 mV【1】, so it suggests the water is less oxygenated than normal and may be experiencing organic loading or other reducing conditions. This is a cause for concern and warrants further investigation.

### Answer C

A reading of +90 mV is below the healthy aerobic range of +200 mV to +400 mV【5†L1-L3】. This suggests the water is in a more reducing state (e.g., lower dissolved‑oxygen or other electron‑accepting conditions) and is not typical of healthy, well‑oxygenated water.
