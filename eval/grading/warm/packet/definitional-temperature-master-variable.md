## Turn 1

**Question:** Why does the reference call temperature the master variable?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- states temperature sets DO capacity — warmer water holds less oxygen
- states it accelerates biological and chemical rates
- states it shifts EC readings (raw EC rises ~2% per °C)
- states the other five parameters should be interpreted relative to temperature

**Must not**
- treats temperature as just one parameter among six with no coupling
- invents a coupling not in the context

**Should cite**
- water-quality-metrics-source-of-truth.pdf

### Answer A

The reference calls temperature the “master variable” because it drives the behavior of all the other core water‑quality parameters. Temperature determines how much oxygen the water can hold (DO capacity), speeds up biological and chemical reactions that consume or produce oxygen, and changes ion mobility, which in turn shifts electrical conductivity (EC) readings. In short, a change in temperature sets the baseline for DO, ORP, pH, EC, and turbidity, so all other metrics must be interpreted relative to the current temperature【5†L1-L8】.

## Turn 2

**Question:** Our temperature rose 3 °C this week and DO fell. Event or expected?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- states a temperature rise should always be paired with an expected DO decline, so the pairing alone is not an event
- points to the thermal-discharge signature (temperature up strongly, DO down proportionally, other chemistry flat) as the thing to rule in or out
- recommends checking EC, pH, ORP and turbidity to separate natural warming from a thermal discharge

**Must not**
- declares a pollution event from temperature and DO alone
- ignores the expected inverse coupling

**Should cite**
- water-quality-metrics-source-of-truth.pdf

### Answer A

A 3 °C rise in temperature will naturally lower the water’s dissolved‑oxygen capacity and can increase respiration rates, so a concurrent drop in DO is the expected, temperature‑driven response. Unless other parameters (e.g., ORP, EC, turbidity) also show abnormal shifts, this pattern is not an event but the normal temperature‑DO coupling described in the reference【5†L1-L8】.

### Answer B

A 3 °C rise in temperature is normal and should be accompanied by a drop in dissolved oxygen. The water‑quality guidance states that “a temperature rise should always be paired with an expected DO decline”【2】. So the observed DO fall is an expected response, not an event.
