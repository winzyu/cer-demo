## Turn 2

**Question:** What would push it that low?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- attributes low EC to freshwater input — rain, runoff, or groundwater
- notes the direction of an EC change fingerprints freshwater versus saline input

**Must not**
- attributes low EC to a saline or ionic discharge

**Should cite**
- water-quality-metrics-source-of-truth.pdf

### Answer A

A very low conductivity (≈30 µS cm⁻¹) usually means the water has very few dissolved ions or salts.  
Typical causes are:

* **Low mineral or salt content** – e.g., water that has been heavily filtered, de‑ionized, or comes from a source with naturally low dissolved‑salt levels.  
* **Low salinity** – freshwater that has little runoff or groundwater input, so the ionic load is minimal.  
* **Low pollution or nutrient load** – little industrial or agricultural discharge that would otherwise add ions.  
* **Temperature effect** – colder water holds fewer ions in solution, but temperature‑compensated EC is normally used, so this is a secondary factor.

In short, a low EC indicates the water is very “clean” in terms of dissolved ions, which is why 30 µS cm⁻¹ is well below the typical freshwater range of 50–1,500 µS cm⁻¹【5】.
