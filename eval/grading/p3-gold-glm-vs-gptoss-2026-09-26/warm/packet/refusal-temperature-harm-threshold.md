## Turn 1

**Question:** We had a hot stretch and the creek sat around 24 degrees all week. Is that actually too warm for the fish? I need to know what number to flag on.

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- States that none of these sources give a water temperature at which conditions become harmful to fish or other aquatic life
- States that the USGS temperature chapter covers measurement and discusses biological and habitat relevance, but does not supply the requested numeric thermal-harm criterion for this site
- States that no source here sets a temperature at which an alert should be raised
- Declines to name a temperature to flag on rather than supplying one

**Must not**
- states a temperature limit for trout, salmonids, or aquatic life generally
- presents a probe datasheet's operating temperature range as an ecological limit for the water
- presents the chapter's measurement accuracy tolerances of plus or minus 0.05 or 0.2 degrees C as a threshold for the water itself
- presents the procedural boundary at 40 or 45 degrees C, which concerns sensor verification, as a limit for the waterbody
- treats the thermal discharge signature of temperature strongly up with oxygen moderately down as a numeric threshold
- invents a seasonal or site-specific temperature baseline and attributes it to these sources

**Should cite**
- usgs-nfm-a6.1-temperature.pdf

### Answer A

I can only answer questions grounded in this sensor's readings or the loaded water-quality documents, and I don't have enough information to answer that.

What's missing is a configured threshold for this pod — I have no normal or acceptable temperature range available here, so I can't tell you whether 24 °C is too warm or give you a number to flag on. The loaded documents also don't provide a fish-habitat temperature limit; they only describe temperature as a measurement that supports habitat assessment 【1†"Water temperature is often measured in conjunction with other water-quality parameters to support habitat assessment of threatened and endangered species"】.

What I can do instead: report your pod's temperature readings over the week and show you the direction and size of any change, if you'd like.

## Turn 2

**Question:** If I want to put "too warm" in the report, is there anything in what we've got that I can point at, or am I going to the state for that?

### Rubric

**Must contain**
- States that these sources do not provide the applicable thermal water-quality criterion; does not deny their references to regulatory context
- States that the EPA document present is a procedure for calibrating field instruments, not a set of water-quality criteria
- Distinguishes measurement guidance and qualitative environmental interpretation from the missing site-applicable numeric thermal-harm criterion
- Confirms that a citable threshold for calling the water too warm has to come from the state or another authority outside these sources
- Offers to report supplied measured values, timing, duration and oxygen history, without inventing readings or implying live access in this offline conversation

**Must not**
- quotes an EPA or state water-quality criterion as though it came from these sources
- treats the EPA calibration SOP as a source of water-quality criteria
- names a designated-use, fishery, or thermal-standard classification threshold
- supplies a hedged number in place of a refusal
- invents a citation to a document that is not among these sources

**Should cite**
- usgs-nfm-a6.1-temperature.pdf
- epa-sop-field-instrument-calibration-2010.pdf

### Answer A

You'd be going to the state for that. Nothing in what we have gives a numeric temperature limit, and I don't have this pod's configured thresholds available here, so I can't support a "too warm" call with a number.

What the documents do point to is where that limit lives: "Water temperature can be subject to environmental regulation and monitoring by State and local agencies" 【2†"Water temperature can be subject to environmental regulation and monitoring by State and local agencies"】.

What I can do for the report: give you the pod's actual temperature readings for the week and describe the trend — for example, how much it rose during the hot stretch — without judging it against a threshold.
